name: Legs Up the Wall
sanskrit:
  iast: Viparīta Karaṇī
  simple: Viparita Karani
alsoKnownAs: [Legs-Up-the-Wall Pose]
summary: A restful pose lying on your back with your legs resting up a wall, letting the whole body be supported while the breath slows.

level: beginner
category: restorative
alsoIn: [supine]
bodyFocus: [legs, hamstrings, spine]
goals: [calm, recovery, sleep]
props: [wall, blanket, bolster]

sided: false
holdGuide: 3–10 minutes, or as long as feels comfortable.
setup: Place your mat with one short end against a wall. Keep a folded blanket nearby for your head or hips.
steps:
  - Sit sideways next to the wall, with one hip and shoulder close to it and your knees bent.
  - Lean back onto your forearms, then swing your legs up the wall as you lower your back onto the mat.
  - Shuffle your hips as close to the wall as feels comfortable. They can stay a little way from the wall if the backs of your legs feel tight.
  - Let your legs rest against the wall, with your knees soft and your feet relaxed.
  - Rest your head on the mat or a thin folded blanket, and let your arms rest by your sides or on your belly.
  - Close your eyes if you like, and let your breath settle into its own rhythm.
  - Stay for a few minutes, letting the wall and the floor support you.
breathCue: There is nothing to do with the breath here. Let it slow down on its own.
comingOut: Bend your knees and slide your feet down the wall towards you. Roll onto your side and rest there for a few breaths, then press up slowly to sitting.

benefits:
  - Lets the legs rest after standing or walking
  - Gently stretches the backs of the legs
  - Many people find it deeply calming, especially in the evening

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Forcing the hips right up against the wall
    fix: Move your hips a little away from the wall until the backs of your legs feel at ease.
  - mistake: Staying when the feet tingle or go numb
    fix: Bend your knees, bring your feet down, and come out to rest on your side.
  - mistake: Sitting up quickly
    fix: Roll onto your side first and pause before coming up slowly.

modifications:
  - Place a folded blanket or a bolster under your hips for gentle lift.
  - Move your hips further from the wall, or bend your knees and rest your feet on the wall.
  - Rest your calves on the seat of a sturdy chair instead of on the wall.
  - Cover yourself with a blanket to stay warm.

Getting into Legs Up the Wall can feel a little clumsy at first. Swinging the legs up from a sideways seat soon becomes familiar.

Once you are there, there is nothing more to do. Let the wall hold your legs and the floor hold the rest of you.
