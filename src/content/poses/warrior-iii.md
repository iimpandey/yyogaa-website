name: Warrior III
sanskrit:
  iast: Vīrabhadrāsana III
  simple: Virabhadrasana III
alsoKnownAs: [Warrior Three, Warrior 3]
summary: A standing balance on one leg, with the body tipping forwards and the other leg reaching back. Easiest to learn with hands on a wall or chair.

level: intermediate
category: balance
alsoIn: [standing]
bodyFocus: [legs, core, glutes, hamstrings]
goals: [strength, focus]
props: [mat, block, wall]

sided: true
holdGuide: 2–5 slow breaths on each side. Short, steady stays are better than long, wobbly ones.
setup: Stand facing a wall or the back of a sturdy chair, about a leg's length away. Begin in Mountain Pose with your hands on your hips.
steps:
  - Shift your weight onto your right foot and keep your right knee softly bent.
  - As you breathe out, hinge forwards from your hips and place your hands on the wall or chair at about hip height.
  - Slowly lift your left leg behind you, only a little at first. It does not need to reach hip height.
  - Keep your hips level, with your left toes pointing down towards the floor rather than turning out.
  - Reach back through your left heel and forwards through the crown of your head, so your body feels long.
  - If you feel steady, you can try lifting your hands away from the support, or resting them on blocks on the floor.
  - Stay for a few breaths, then come out and repeat on the other side.
breathCue: Breathe slowly and evenly. Each breath in can lengthen you from heel to head. Each breath out can steady the standing leg.
comingOut: Lower your back leg, step it forwards to meet the standing foot and rise slowly to Mountain Pose. Pause for a few breaths before the second side.

benefits:
  - Builds strength in the standing leg, the buttocks and the core
  - Challenges balance and encourages focus
  - Stretches the back of the standing leg

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Locking the standing knee
    fix: Keep a soft bend in your standing knee throughout.
  - mistake: Lifting the back leg as high as possible
    fix: Lift it only a little. Level hips matter more than a high leg.
  - mistake: Letting the lifted hip roll open towards the ceiling
    fix: Point the toes of your lifted foot down to the floor to help keep your hips level.
  - mistake: Practising without support while learning
    fix: Use a wall or a chair until the shape feels steady and familiar.

modifications:
  - Keep your hands on a wall or the back of a sturdy chair the whole time.
  - Rest your hands on blocks placed under your shoulders.
  - Keep the toes of your back foot lightly touching the floor instead of lifting the leg.
  - Keep your hands on your hips and tip forwards only a little.

Warrior III asks for strength and balance at the same time, which is why it is marked as intermediate. Using a wall or a chair is not a shortcut. It lets you learn the shape safely.

Wobbling is part of balancing. If you tip, simply step down, pause, and begin again.
