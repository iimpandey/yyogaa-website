name: Seated Twist
sanskrit:
  iast: Parivṛtta Sukhāsana
  simple: Parivrtta Sukhasana
alsoKnownAs: [Easy Seated Twist, Simple Seated Twist]
summary: A gentle cross-legged twist that turns the upper body to one side, growing tall as you breathe in and turning a little as you breathe out.

level: beginner
category: twist
alsoIn: [seated]
bodyFocus: [spine, side-body, shoulders]
goals: [mobility, posture, calm]
props: [mat, blanket]

sided: true
holdGuide: 3–6 slow breaths on each side.
setup: Sit in Easy Pose on the edge of a folded blanket, so your hips are a little higher than your knees.
steps:
  - Sit tall, with your shoulders relaxed and your hands resting on your knees.
  - Place your left hand on your right knee and your right fingertips on the floor or blanket behind you.
  - As you breathe in, lengthen up through your spine.
  - As you breathe out, turn your belly and chest gently to the right, starting from the middle of your back.
  - Let your arms simply rest where they are. Never use them to pull yourself further round.
  - Let your head follow the turn last, only as far as feels easy on your neck.
  - Stay for a few breaths, then come back to the centre and repeat on the other side.
breathCue: Breathe in to grow tall. Breathe out to turn a little, or simply to rest where you are.
comingOut: As you breathe in, slowly turn back to face forwards, leading with your head and chest. Pause in the centre for a breath, then practise the second side.

benefits:
  - Invites gentle turning movement through the middle and upper back
  - Stretches the sides of the waist and the shoulders
  - Encourages a tall, easy posture
  - Many people find it a calm way to begin or end a practice

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Yanking the body round with the arms
    fix: Let your hands rest lightly. The turn comes from your back and breath, not your arms.
  - mistake: Slumping as you turn
    fix: Lengthen up on every breath in before turning any further.
  - mistake: Turning the head first and furthest
    fix: Turn your belly and chest first, and let your neck follow gently last.

modifications:
  - Sit on a higher folded blanket or a firm cushion.
  - Sit sideways on a sturdy chair and turn towards the chair back, holding it lightly.
  - Sit with your legs stretched out in front of you if crossing your legs is uncomfortable.
  - Keep the twist very small, turning only your chest.

Seated Twist is a small, simple movement that fits almost anywhere: at the start of a practice, at the end, or in a break from sitting at a desk.

Think of growing taller as much as turning. The twist will usually find its own comfortable depth.
