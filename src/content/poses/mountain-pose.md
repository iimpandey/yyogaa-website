name: Mountain Pose
sanskrit:
  iast: Tāḍāsana
  simple: Tadasana
alsoKnownAs: []
summary: A simple, steady standing shape that helps you notice how you stand, share your weight evenly and breathe with ease.

level: beginner
category: standing
alsoIn: []
bodyFocus: [legs, feet, core]
goals: [posture, focus, beginner-friendly]
props: [none]

sided: false
holdGuide: 5–10 slow breaths.
setup: Stand at the front of your mat with your feet about hip-width apart and your arms resting by your sides.
steps:
  - Let your feet point forward and spread your toes gently.
  - Feel your weight spread evenly between the heels and the balls of both feet.
  - Keep your knees soft rather than locked.
  - Let your tailbone point gently down, so your lower back is neither arched nor tucked under.
  - Lift gently through the crown of your head and let your shoulders relax down and back.
  - Let your arms hang naturally, with your palms facing your thighs or turned slightly forward.
  - Rest your gaze softly ahead and breathe steadily.
breathCue: Breathe in and feel yourself grow a little taller. Breathe out and let your shoulders settle.
comingOut: Simply relax your body, shake out your legs if you like, and move on to your next pose.

benefits:
  - Builds awareness of how you stand and carry your weight
  - Invites steady, gentle effort through the legs and the middle of the body
  - Offers a calm, grounded place to begin or end a practice

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Locking the knees
    fix: Keep a tiny softness in your knees so your legs are active but not jammed straight.
  - mistake: Leaning back into the heels
    fix: Rock gently forward and back until your weight feels even across both feet.
  - mistake: Lifting the chest by arching the lower back
    fix: Let your front ribs soften down and think of lengthening the back of your waist.
  - mistake: Holding the shoulders stiffly
    fix: Lift your shoulders up towards your ears, then let them drop and rest.

modifications:
  - Stand with your feet a little wider apart for a steadier base.
  - Stand with your back near a wall to feel where your head, shoulders and hips are.
  - If standing is tiring today, sit tall on a sturdy chair with your feet flat on the floor.

Mountain Pose can look like simply standing still, and in a way it is. It is a chance to notice your habits: where your weight falls, which shoulder lifts, how you breathe.

Many other standing poses begin and end here, so it is worth getting to know.
