name: Savasana
sanskrit:
  iast: Śavāsana
  simple: Savasana
alsoKnownAs: [Corpse Pose, Relaxation Pose]
summary: A still, restful pose lying on your back, usually at the end of a practice, to let the body settle and the breath slow.

level: beginner
category: restorative
alsoIn: [supine]
bodyFocus: [spine, shoulders, neck]
goals: [calm, recovery, beginner-friendly]
props: [mat, blanket]

sided: false
holdGuide: 3–10 minutes, or as long as feels right. Even one minute is worthwhile.
setup: Lie on your back on your mat. Keep a blanket nearby, as the body often cools as it rests.
steps:
  - Let your legs rest a comfortable distance apart and allow your feet to fall outwards.
  - Rest your arms a little away from your sides, with your palms facing up or down, whichever feels more comfortable.
  - Adjust anything that feels awkward, then let yourself become still.
  - Close your eyes if you like, or rest your gaze softly on the ceiling.
  - Let your breath find its own natural rhythm, without trying to change it.
  - Notice the weight of your body resting on the floor, and let the floor hold you.
breathCue: There is nothing to do with the breath here. Simply let it come and go on its own.
comingOut: Let your breath deepen, gently move your fingers and toes, then bend your knees and roll onto your side. Pause there before pressing up slowly to sitting.

benefits:
  - Gives you time to rest and let your practice settle
  - Many people find it calming
  - Helps you practise being still and noticing the breath

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Skipping it
    fix: Even a minute or two of rest counts. It is part of the practice, not an extra.
  - mistake: Staying in an uncomfortable position
    fix: Take a moment to adjust. Add a blanket, or a cushion under your knees or head.
  - mistake: Getting up too quickly
    fix: Roll onto your side first and come up slowly.

modifications:
  - Place a rolled blanket or a bolster under your knees to ease your lower back.
  - Rest your head on a thin folded blanket.
  - Cover yourself with a blanket to stay warm.
  - If lying on your back is uncomfortable, rest on your side with a cushion between your knees.

Savasana can be surprisingly hard. Staying still without doing anything is a skill, and like other skills it grows with practice.

If your mind is busy, that is normal. Notice it kindly and come back to the feeling of the breath.
