name: Downward-Facing Dog
sanskrit:
  iast: Adho Mukha Śvānāsana
  simple: Adho Mukha Svanasana
alsoKnownAs: [Down Dog, Downward Dog]
summary: An upside-down V shape that gently lengthens the back and the backs of the legs while your weight is shared between hands and feet.

level: beginner
category: standing
alsoIn: [inversion]
bodyFocus: [hamstrings, shoulders, spine]
goals: [flexibility, strength, beginner-friendly]
props: [mat]

sided: false
holdGuide: 3–5 slow breaths to begin with. Stay longer only if it feels comfortable.
setup: Come onto your hands and knees, with your hands a little in front of your shoulders and your knees under your hips.
steps:
  - Spread your fingers comfortably wide and press the whole of each palm into the mat.
  - Curl your toes under so the balls of your feet rest on the mat.
  - As you breathe out, lift your knees away from the mat and send your hips up and back.
  - Keep your knees softly bent at first, so your back can stay long rather than rounded.
  - Let your head hang between your upper arms, with your neck relaxed and your gaze towards your knees.
  - If it feels comfortable, let your heels sink a little towards the mat. They do not need to touch it.
  - Stay for a few slow breaths, keeping your weight shared evenly between your hands and feet.
breathCue: Breathe in and feel your spine grow long. Breathe out and let your shoulders soften away from your ears.
comingOut: Bend your knees, lower them gently to the mat, and sit back towards your heels to rest for a few breaths.

benefits:
  - Stretches the backs of the legs and the calves
  - Invites length through the spine
  - Builds steady strength in the arms and shoulders
  - Offers a familiar place to pause and breathe between other poses

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Straightening the legs even though the back rounds
    fix: Bend your knees as much as you need. A long back matters more here than straight legs.
  - mistake: Letting the shoulders creep up towards the ears
    fix: Press the mat away with your hands and let your shoulder blades widen across your back.
  - mistake: Letting the weight sink into the heels of the hands
    fix: Press through your fingertips and the base of each finger too, so the load is spread across the whole hand.
  - mistake: Holding the breath
    fix: Keep the breath slow and easy. If you cannot breathe comfortably, ease off or come down and rest.

modifications:
  - Keep your knees bent the whole time. This is a complete version of the pose, not a lesser one.
  - Place your hands on the seat of a sturdy chair set against a wall, then walk your feet back until your body makes a gentle upside-down L shape.
  - Take shorter stays of two or three breaths, resting on your knees in between.

cautions:
  - If your wrists, shoulders or the backs of your legs feel strained, bend your knees more, try the chair version, or rest.

  - Move into and out of the pose slowly, especially if you are not used to having your head lower than your heart.

Many people meet Downward-Facing Dog in one of their first classes, often as a pause between other movements.

It can feel quite different from one day to the next: stiffer in the morning, easier after a walk. There is no finished version to reach. Let the shape meet you where you are today.
