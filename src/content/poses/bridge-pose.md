name: Bridge Pose
sanskrit:
  iast: Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana
  simple: Setu Bandha Sarvangasana
alsoKnownAs: [Bridge]
summary: A gentle backbend lying on your back, lifting the hips from the floor while the feet, arms and shoulders stay grounded.

level: beginner
category: backbend
alsoIn: [supine]
bodyFocus: [glutes, legs, spine, chest]
goals: [strength, mobility, energy]
props: [mat]

sided: false
holdGuide: 3–5 slow breaths, then lower. Repeat two or three times if it feels comfortable.
setup: Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart and close enough that your fingertips can almost touch your heels. Rest your arms by your sides, palms down.
steps:
  - Press your feet and arms gently into the floor.
  - As you breathe out, slowly lift your hips away from the mat.
  - Lift only as high as feels comfortable, keeping your weight on your feet and shoulders, not your neck.
  - Keep your knees pointing forward, in line with your hips and feet.
  - Keep your head still and look straight up. Do not turn your head while your hips are lifted.
  - Stay for a few breaths, keeping the breath steady.
breathCue: Breathe out as you lift your hips. Breathe steadily while you stay, and breathe out again as you lower.
comingOut: Breathe out and roll your spine slowly back down to the mat, from your upper back to your hips. Rest with your knees bent.

benefits:
  - Builds strength in the backs of the legs and the buttocks
  - Invites gentle opening across the fronts of the hips and the chest
  - Offers a gentle introduction to backbends

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Letting the knees drift apart or fall together
    fix: Keep your knees in line with your hips. It can help to imagine holding a small cushion between them.
  - mistake: Turning the head while the hips are lifted
    fix: Keep your head still. Come down first if you need to look around.
  - mistake: Lifting the hips too high and arching the lower back
    fix: Lift a little lower and let the front of your ribs soften.
  - mistake: Placing the feet too far away
    fix: Walk your feet a little closer to your hips.

modifications:
  - Lift your hips just a few centimetres, then lower. Repeat slowly with your breath.
  - Rest the flat back of your pelvis on a firm block or cushion, and stay there with support.
  - Keep your hips on the mat and simply press your feet down, feeling the backs of your legs work.

Bridge Pose can be practised as a slow movement, lifting and lowering with the breath, or held for a few breaths. Both are useful.

Keep the lift modest at first. A low, comfortable bridge is still Bridge Pose.
