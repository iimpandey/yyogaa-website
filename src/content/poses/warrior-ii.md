name: Warrior II
sanskrit:
  iast: Vīrabhadrāsana II
  simple: Virabhadrasana II
alsoKnownAs: [Warrior Two, Warrior 2]
summary: A strong, open standing pose with the legs wide, the front knee bent and the arms reaching out, practised on both sides.

level: beginner
category: standing
alsoIn: []
bodyFocus: [legs, hips, shoulders]
goals: [strength, focus]
props: [mat]

sided: true
holdGuide: 3–5 slow breaths on each side to begin with.
setup: Stand facing the long edge of your mat and step your feet wide apart, roughly the length of one of your legs.
steps:
  - Turn your right foot out so your toes point to the short end of the mat, and angle your left toes slightly inwards.
  - Line up your right heel roughly with the middle of your left foot.
  - Raise your arms out to the sides at shoulder height, with your palms facing down.
  - As you breathe out, bend your right knee so it moves towards being over your right ankle.
  - Keep your right knee pointing the same way as your right toes, rather than letting it fall inwards.
  - Keep your back leg straight and strong, pressing through the outer edge of your back foot.
  - Keep your body upright, with your shoulders over your hips, and look out past your right hand.
  - Stay for a few breaths, then come out and repeat on the other side.
breathCue: Breathe in to lengthen up through your spine and arms. Breathe out to settle into the front leg, only as far as feels steady.
comingOut: Straighten your front leg, lower your arms, and turn your feet to face the long edge of the mat. Rest for a breath, then practise the second side.

benefits:
  - Builds steady strength in the legs
  - Invites the hips and inner thighs to open gently
  - Encourages focus and a sense of steadiness
  - Asks the arms and shoulders to stay active

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Letting the front knee drop inwards
    fix: Guide your front knee to point over your middle toes, and bend it less if you need to.
  - mistake: Letting the front knee move past the ankle
    fix: Take a slightly wider stance, or bend the knee a little less.
  - mistake: Leaning the body towards the front leg
    fix: Bring your shoulders back over your hips, as if your back were resting against a wall.
  - mistake: Lifting the shoulders towards the ears
    fix: Let your shoulders drop and keep your arms long rather than tense.

modifications:
  - Take a shorter stance and bend your front knee only a little.
  - Rest your hands on your hips instead of lifting your arms.
  - Stand near a wall or a sturdy chair so you can steady yourself with one hand.
  - Hold for fewer breaths and rest between sides.

Warrior II is a pose of steady effort rather than strain. It can help to think of being both grounded and spacious: feet rooted, arms reaching wide.

Your two sides may feel quite different. That is common, and it is one reason the pose is practised on both.
