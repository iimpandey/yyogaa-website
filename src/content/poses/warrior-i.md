name: Warrior I
sanskrit:
  iast: Vīrabhadrāsana I
  simple: Virabhadrasana I
alsoKnownAs: [Warrior One, Warrior 1]
summary: A steady standing lunge facing the front of the mat, with the front knee bent, the back leg long and the arms reaching up.

level: beginner
category: standing
alsoIn: []
bodyFocus: [legs, hips, shoulders]
goals: [strength, focus]
props: [mat]

sided: true
holdGuide: 3–5 slow breaths on each side to begin with.
setup: Stand at the front of your mat in Mountain Pose, with your feet hip-width apart and your hands on your hips.
steps:
  - Step your left foot back a comfortable distance. A shorter stance is steadier, so there is no need to step far.
  - Turn your left foot out slightly and press the back heel down, or keep it lifted if that feels easier on your back leg.
  - Keep your feet roughly hip-width apart from side to side, as if standing on two train tracks rather than a tightrope.
  - As you breathe out, bend your right knee so it moves towards being over your right ankle, pointing the same way as your toes.
  - Let your hips face forwards as much as is comfortable. They do not need to be fully square to the front.
  - Keep your hands on your hips, or as you breathe in, raise your arms up with your shoulders relaxed.
  - Stay for a few breaths, then come out and repeat on the other side.
breathCue: Breathe in to lengthen up through your spine and arms. Breathe out to settle a little into the front leg, only as far as feels steady.
comingOut: Lower your arms, straighten your front leg and step your back foot forwards to meet the front one. Pause in Mountain Pose, then practise the second side.

benefits:
  - Builds steady strength in the legs
  - Stretches the front of the back hip
  - Encourages focus and a sense of standing tall
  - Asks the arms and shoulders to stay active

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Taking a very long stance
    fix: Shorten your stance until you feel steady and your back leg is comfortable.
  - mistake: Letting the front knee drop inwards
    fix: Guide your front knee to point over your middle toes, and bend it less if you need to.
  - mistake: Forcing the hips to face fully forwards
    fix: Let your back hip angle a little. Comfort in your lower back matters more than square hips.
  - mistake: Lifting the shoulders towards the ears
    fix: Keep your hands on your hips, or widen your arms into a gentle V shape.

modifications:
  - Keep your hands on your hips the whole time.
  - Keep your back heel lifted, so the stance feels more like a high lunge.
  - Stand next to a wall or a sturdy chair so you can steady yourself with one hand.
  - Hold for fewer breaths and rest in Mountain Pose between sides.

Warrior I can feel like a pose of quiet determination: feet grounded, spine tall, arms reaching up.

The shape changes a lot with the length of your stance. If it feels like a struggle, step your feet a little closer together and let the pose become easier.
