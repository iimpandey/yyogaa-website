name: Child's Pose
sanskrit:
  iast: Bālāsana
  simple: Balasana
alsoKnownAs: [Child Pose, Resting Pose]
summary: A kneeling, folded-forward resting shape that lets you pause, breathe into your back and settle between other poses.

level: beginner
category: restorative
alsoIn: [forward-fold]
bodyFocus: [hips, spine, shoulders]
goals: [calm, recovery, beginner-friendly]
props: [mat]

sided: false
holdGuide: 5–10 slow breaths, or for as long as it stays comfortable.
setup: Kneel on your mat with your big toes close together and your knees about hip-width apart, or wider if that feels better.
steps:
  - Sit your hips back towards your heels. They do not need to touch.
  - Walk your hands forward along the mat and let your chest lower towards or between your thighs.
  - Rest your forehead on the mat, a folded blanket or a block.
  - Let your arms rest where they feel easiest, either reaching forward or alongside your body with the palms up.
  - Let your shoulders, jaw and belly soften.
  - Stay here and breathe slowly, feeling your back gently rise and fall.
breathCue: Breathe into the back of your ribs, and let each out-breath help you settle a little more.
comingOut: Walk your hands back towards your knees and slowly roll up to sitting, lifting your head last.

benefits:
  - Offers a calm, familiar place to rest during a practice
  - Gently lengthens the back
  - Invites a soft stretch around the hips and the fronts of the thighs
  - Many people find it helps them slow the breath down

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Forcing the hips down to the heels
    fix: Let your hips rest wherever they settle, or place a cushion between your heels and hips.
  - mistake: Letting the neck take weight
    fix: Rest your forehead on something that lets your neck stay relaxed, such as a block or a folded blanket.
  - mistake: Holding tension in the shoulders
    fix: Try the arms alongside your body with the palms up, and let your shoulders drape towards the mat.

modifications:
  - Place a folded blanket or cushion between your calves and thighs if your knees or ankles feel tight.
  - Rest your chest on a bolster or a stack of pillows placed lengthways between your knees.
  - Widen your knees to make more room for your belly and chest.
  - If kneeling is not comfortable today, lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet on the floor instead.

Child's Pose is often offered as a place to rest at any point in a practice. You never need permission to come into it.

Some days it feels deeply comfortable; other days your knees or ankles may ask for more padding. Both are fine. Adjust it until your breath feels easy.
