name: Cat-Cow
sanskrit:
  iast: Mārjaryāsana–Bitilāsana
  simple: Marjaryasana-Bitilasana
alsoKnownAs: [Cat and Cow, Cat-Cow Stretch]
summary: A slow, flowing movement on hands and knees that gently rounds and arches the spine in time with the breath.

level: beginner
category: backbend
alsoIn: []
bodyFocus: [spine, core, neck]
goals: [mobility, calm, beginner-friendly]
props: [mat]

sided: false
holdGuide: 5–10 slow rounds, moving with your breath.
setup: Come onto your hands and knees, with your hands under your shoulders and your knees under your hips.
steps:
  - Spread your fingers and press evenly through your hands. Start with your back in a comfortable, neutral position.
  - As you breathe in, let your belly lower gently towards the mat, lift your chest forward and let your gaze move a little forward. This is Cow.
  - As you breathe out, press the mat away, round your back towards the ceiling, and let your head and tailbone curl down. This is Cat.
  - Keep moving slowly between the two shapes, letting your breath lead the movement.
  - Keep the movements small and comfortable, especially in your neck and lower back.
  - After your last round, return to a neutral back.
breathCue: Breathe in as you arch into Cow. Breathe out as you round into Cat.
comingOut: Return to a neutral back, then sit back towards your heels to rest in Child's Pose, or come up to sitting.

benefits:
  - Gently moves the whole spine
  - Offers a soft way to warm up at the start of a practice
  - Helps you link movement with the breath
  - Many people find the steady rhythm calming

commonMistakes:
  - mistake: Moving too quickly
    fix: Let each movement last as long as the breath that goes with it.
  - mistake: Throwing the head back in Cow
    fix: Lift your chest rather than your chin, and keep the back of your neck long.
  - mistake: Sinking into the lower back
    fix: Keep a little support from your belly so the arch spreads along the whole spine.

modifications:
  - Place a folded blanket under your knees for padding.
  - Come onto your forearms, or make soft fists, if your wrists feel sensitive.
  - Sit on a sturdy chair with your hands on your knees, and gently arch and round your back in time with your breath.

Cat-Cow pairs two simple shapes into one gentle movement. It is often used as a warm-up, but it can also be a short practice of its own on a stiff morning.

There is no need to make big shapes. Small, easy movements that follow your breath are enough.
